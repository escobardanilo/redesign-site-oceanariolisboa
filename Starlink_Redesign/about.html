About

Connecting the whole planet, not just the parts already covered

Starlink is a satellite internet constellation in low Earth orbit, built to deliver fast, reliable broadband to places fiber and cable never reached — and to keep working when they fail.

Mission

Internet access shouldn't depend on your zip code

Traditional internet infrastructure is expensive to build and maintain across mountains, oceans and sparsely populated regions — so billions of people have been left with slow connections or none at all. Starlink was built to close that gap: instead of laying cable, it puts a dense mesh of small satellites in low Earth orbit, close enough to the ground for fast, low-latency service, and numerous enough to cover virtually the entire globe.

Starlink is developed and operated by SpaceX, using the same launch infrastructure and engineering discipline built for orbital spaceflight — which is part of why the network has scaled so quickly since its first satellites reached orbit.

Milestones

From first launch to a global network

2002

SpaceX is founded

The company that would go on to build and launch the Starlink constellation begins as a rocket manufacturer and launch provider.

2019

First operational satellites reach orbit

The first large batch of production Starlink satellites launches aboard a Falcon 9, beginning the build-out of the constellation.

2020

Public beta opens

Early users in select regions get their first hands-on connections, feeding back real-world performance data.

2021–22

Residential service expands worldwide

Coverage grows across a widening list of countries, with hardware and installation getting simpler with every revision.

Since

Roam, Maritime, Aviation and Business launch

The same network extends beyond homes: to vehicles, vessels, aircraft and enterprises that need connectivity anywhere they operate.
