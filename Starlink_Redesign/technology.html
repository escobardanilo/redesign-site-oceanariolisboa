Technology

A satellite network built for low latency, not just coverage

Most legacy satellite internet relies on a handful of satellites parked in high, distant orbits. Starlink takes the opposite approach: thousands of small satellites, flying close to Earth.

How it works

From orbit to your router, in four steps

Low Earth orbit satellites

Flying roughly 550 km up — a fraction of the distance of traditional satellite internet — which cuts signal travel time dramatically and keeps latency close to that of ground-based broadband.

Laser inter-satellite links

Satellites relay traffic between each other over laser links, routing data across the constellation before it ever has to touch the ground — useful over oceans and remote regions with no local ground station.

Phased-array user terminal

The dish has no moving parts. It electronically steers a beam to track satellites as they pass overhead, handing off from one to the next automatically and continuously.

Ground stations and the wider internet

Traffic comes down to a ground station connected to the global internet backbone, completing the round trip in tens of milliseconds rather than the half-second-plus typical of older satellite services.
